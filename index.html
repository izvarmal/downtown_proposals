Inside ASSESS

A report every business owner can act on.

Each Storefront Performance Scorecard is seven pages, generated from what we record on the street and reviewed by us before it goes out. It turns a storefront into a short list of decisions.

  1. The score. Performance out of 100 across five vital signs: upper facade, sign, windows, entrance, and lighting.
  2. What it means. What the score costs the business in visibility, invitation, upkeep, brand, and its effect on the block.
  3. Fix first. Every finding ranked, critical before watch, with the specific fix for each.
  4. The prescription. One big design decision, a paint palette matched to commercial paints, the sign text, and the window strategy.
  5. The budget. A concept budget for the work, with ranges and contingency, so owners know what to apply for.
  6. The checklist. One list to hand every painter, sign maker, and contractor before they quote.

Inside ACTIVATE

Five strategies. Six parts each. Ready to present.

The Idea Factory report turns your assessment into five moves your downtown can make. Every strategy follows the same six parts, so a board can compare them side by side and pick where to start.

  1. The issue. A problem your own data shows, stated in one sentence.
  2. The winning asset. Something your downtown already has that can carry the fix.
  3. The big idea. One clear move that puts that asset to work.
  4. The toolkit. The approach and tools we recommend to carry it out.
  5. The action plan. The first steps, in order, so the idea leaves the page.
  6. The quick win. Something visible you can deliver early to show results and justify the next budget.

Why Storefront Mastery

A storefront does more work than most people see.

Whether a downtown business draws people in depends on five things working together. We look at all five, for each business and for the district as a whole.

Visibility

Can people find the business from the sidewalk, the car, and the phone?

Communication

Do the signs, windows, and facade say clearly what is inside and who it is for?

Customer experience

Does the entrance invite people in, and does the visit give them a reason to return?

Commercial identity

Does the business look like itself, and does the street read as a place with a character?

Business and downtown

Does each storefront help its neighbors and the district, or work against them?

Questions

Common questions.

Which program is right for us?

Start with ALIGN if your board, staff, and businesses do not yet agree on priorities. Go to ASSESS if you are about to fund storefront improvements or a facade program and want to know where the money will do the most good. Go to ACTIVATE if you want a district strategy your partners can act on. Many districts need all three; each tier includes the one before it.

If you are unsure, tell us what you are trying to decide and we will recommend one.

What exactly is included?

Each tier card lists every deliverable. ASSESS includes the ALIGN workshop, and ACTIVATE includes everything in ASSESS. Nothing is billed by the hour, and the price does not change unless the scope, travel, or schedule changes in writing.

Are design services included?

Each ASSESS scorecard includes a design prescription for that storefront: one big decision, a palette, the sign text, and a window strategy. Full design direction for a storefront is a separate service, the Storefront Accelerator, from $2,950 per storefront. Architectural drawings, construction documents, and permit sets are not included in any tier.

Can we start small and move up?

Yes. The ALIGN fee is credited in full toward ASSESS or ACTIVATE within 6 months, and the ASSESS fee is credited in full toward ACTIVATE within 12 months. You never pay twice for the same work.

How does an engagement begin?

Email us with your district's name and the street you have in mind. We look at the corridor, confirm which tier fits, and send a fixed-price proposal. Work is scheduled once the agreement is signed. Engagements can be contracted by a district management corporation, a nonprofit, or a municipality.
