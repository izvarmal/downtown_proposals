What happens without a plan

Ten businesses, ten checks, and no shared idea of what the money should fix.

Everyone asks for a sign

Owners request the upgrade they can picture. The sign gets funded while the dark window, the dead entrance and the peeling trim stay as they are.

Three quotes for three different jobs

Your program asks for three estimates. Without a written scope, each contractor bids on different work and nobody can compare them.

The fund runs dry mid-list

First come, first served rewards the fastest applicant. The strongest projects show up late and find the pool already spent.

The board asks what it bought

At year end you report to your board, your council or your funder. You need to show what changed on the street and why those projects won.

The deliverable

One report for every storefront in the program.

We score each storefront on the five dimensions we use across every district we work in: legibility, invitation, brand coherence, district contribution and implementation viability. Then we tell the owner what to do first and what it should cost.

In every report

Storefront assessment

  • Five-dimension score on a 5 to 25 scale, with a performance band from critical to high performer
  • Upgrades ranked by impact, so the first dollar goes to the first fix
  • A planning-level cost range for every upgrade
  • What to leave off the grant, and why
  • A color palette matched to paint codes

Questions

What directors ask before they book.

Can we pay for this out of the grant itself?

Often, yes. Many programs allow administration or technical assistance costs, and some state funds budget for a paid program administrator. Check your grant agreement. We write the scope in your funder's language so it is easy to approve.

Do the reports include designs or drawings?

No. These are assessments, not design projects. Each report gives scores, ranked upgrades and planning-level costs. It does not include drawings, construction documentation, renderings or technical specifications. Permits, drawings and installation go through your contractors and licensed professionals.

What if an owner disagrees with the ranking?

Owners choose which ranked upgrades to pursue. The ranking and the cost ranges stay as written, and your committee decides what the grant covers.

Where do the cost ranges come from?

From storefront projects we have guided and regional pricing. They frame the three quotes your program already asks for, so you can spot the one that is off.

Do you rescore the storefronts after the work?

No. The assessment sets the plan before the money goes out. The reports double as the baseline you report against.
